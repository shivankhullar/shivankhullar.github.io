abstract: |-
  We present a radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) cosmological zoom-in simulation of Pop III star formation, drawing initial conditions from a Milky Way-like progenitor halo at redshift *z* ∼ 21. We follow the collapse of primordial gas over 10 orders of magnitude, through to the formation of an accretion disk that becomes gravitationally unstable Δ*t* ∼ −3 kyr before the first protostar's formation. Prominent spiral arms then develop and fragment vigorously, producing ∼60 sink formation events over the first 8 kyr post-collapse evolution, with a total stellar mass reaching &gt;150 M<sub>☉</sub> by 15 kyr, the end of our simulation. The disk remains magnetically supercritical throughout: the mass-to-flux ratio falls from μ<sub>Φ</sub> ∼ 10<sup>2</sup>–10<sup>3</sup> to ∼15 by 15 kyr, as the inflow brings in magnetic flux. The magnetic energy in the disk remains sub-dominant throughout, but grows by ∼3.5 orders of magnitude after the first star forms. The disk starts sub-virial (2*E*<sub>kin</sub>/|*E*<sub>pot</sub>| ≈ 0.6) and reaches virial equilibrium within ∼2 kyr. The total stellar mass of the population grows approximately as *M*<sub>⋆</sub> ∝ *t*<sup>2</sup>, confirming the prediction of accelerating accretion in self-gravitating turbulent collapse, at rates of *Ṁ* ∼ 10<sup>−3</sup>–10<sup>−2</sup> M<sub>☉</sub> yr<sup>−1</sup>. The star formation efficiency reaches ≈90% within the disk (after ≈11 kyr; ≈80% at 9.5 kyr) and ∼50% within a 0.1 pc sphere. We find a top heavy IMF (*dN*/*dm* ∝ *m*<sup>−α</sup>) with a high-mass slope α ≈ 1. The cosmological environment sets the boundary conditions of the disk: an envelope delivers ∼3 × 10<sup>−3</sup> M<sub>☉</sub> yr<sup>−1</sup> to the outer edge of a disk with a radius of ∼10<sup>3</sup> AU; the disk spreads and, in our simulation, its fragmentation sites move outward, from ∼500 to ∼1350 AU, until it has been depleted.
